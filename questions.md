# Questions for the Orion's Arm community

This map is built from the Encyclopaedia Galactica, the Celestia add-on, the Inner Sphere colony tables and the political maps of the Middle Regions, cross-checked against real astronomical catalogues. Where those disagree, or where a source locates something too loosely to draw, the disagreement is recorded here rather than quietly resolved.

Nothing below is a complaint about the setting. Most of it is the ordinary friction of turning prose into coordinates, and a fair number of the items are our own gaps rather than the setting's — those are marked as such.

**Generated by `oastarmap build`.** The counted sections come straight from the data and will change as it does; the written ones are in `fiction/questions.yaml`.

## Open questions (7)

### Enigma system — Three sources put the Enigma system in three different directions.

*contradiction*

The Celestia add-on carries a star named Enigma at RA 81.7, Dec -37.5, 1,410 pc — that is in Columba, 4,598 ly out. The EG article for Stanislaw says Canis Major, whose nearest boundary is some fifteen degrees away; Columba is not a boundary case for it. Separately, the Enigma Cluster is canonically NGC 6755, which sits 3,478 pc from the add-on's star and roughly opposite it on the sky. No star in the add-on comes within 1,549 pc of NGC 6755.

**What the map does now.** Stanislaw is placed by constellation in Canis Major at the add-on's distance, and marked uncertain. The add-on's direction is not used.

**What would settle it.** Which of the three is canonical, or whether the add-on's Enigma is a different object that happens to share the name.

- https://www.orionsarm.com/eg-article/491aecf1478f9

### Celestia add-on numbering — Some of the add-on's "JD" designations in NGC 6633 are HD numbers of real stars, not Orion's Arm's own numbering.

*finding*

A positional cross-match against the real star catalogue at one arcminute finds JD 169512 sitting 0.0 arcmin from HD 169512, and the same for JD 169842, JD 170054, JD 170200 and JD 170293. Five exact digit matches at zero separation is not coincidence. Whether the rule holds for all 52 entries in that cluster group is untested — most of the rest are not in our catalogue at all, so there is nothing to match them against.

**What the map does now.** All 52 are hidden, so nothing is drawn twice. The `oa_designation` flag still treats a JD prefix as Orion's Arm numbering, which for these is wrong.

**What would settle it.** Whether the add-on's NGC 6633 file was built from HD numbers throughout, and whether JD elsewhere in the add-on means anything different.

### Muuh — Do the Muuh have polities in the sense terragen civilisation does?

*category*

Muuhome is classified as Xenosophont rather than under a named polity, because the Muuh are independent — they have their own System of Response and need no terragen protection — but whether they organise into anything answering to an empire is not something we found stated.

**What the map does now.** Recorded with kind `xenosophont` rather than `meta-empire`, so the map does not assert a political structure the setting may not have.

**What would settle it.** Whether "Muuh" should name a polity, or stay a clade with no polity of its own.

- https://www.orionsarm.com/eg-article/47bb872326fa1

### Corambytia Protectorate — The direction uncertainty is larger than the protectorate itself.

*precision*

Given as 290 ly across, 1,500 ly from Sol, in Antlia. Antlia's enclosing radius is 13 degrees, so at that distance the direction is uncertain by about 350 ly — more than the volume's own diameter. The sphere the map draws is the right size in demonstrably the wrong place.

**What the map does now.** Drawn at the constellation centre with an uncertainty circle around it, so the error is visible rather than implied.

**What would settle it.** Coordinates, or a nearby landmark to hang the direction on.

- https://www.orionsarm.com/eg-article/46412ddb422d6

### Evermore — Evermore is described but not located.

*unlocated*

Placed "at the edge of the Disarchy", and the Disarchy is itself given by landmarks rather than coordinates — a volume stretching outward from Lynga 2 toward Feinstein 1 and the SN 1006 nebula, with incursions Solward. Lynga 2 and Feinstein 1 are both in our open cluster catalogue, so that axis could be drawn; the remnant of SN 1006 is not, and "the edge" is not a point in any case.

**What the map does now.** Recorded with no position, and not drawn.

**What would settle it.** A distance and a direction, or a system to bind it to.

- https://www.orionsarm.com/eg-article/48fbc25f56967

### Wadai — Wadai's star is real but in none of our catalogues, so the Inner Sphere table's row for it cannot be bound.

*data-gap*

Wadai appears twice — in the Celestia add-on as EG 471 at 50.5 ly, and in the Inner Sphere table as WD 0230-144 at 54.4 ly. Both are the white dwarf GJ 3162. At absolute magnitude 14.8 it is in neither Hipparcos nor the Bright Star Catalogue, and the nearest star we hold to its position is 26 arcmin away at 423 pc.

**What the map does now.** Drawn at the add-on's position, marked as a real object rather than an asserted one, since the add-on is acting as a catalogue of last resort.

**What would settle it.** Nothing on the fiction side. This is ours to fix by adding a white dwarf catalogue.

- https://www.orionsarm.com/eg-article/4910e5c60edcc

### Darwin / Aristotle — Aristotle is Orion's Arm's name for the star, not a second world.

*reading*

Given to us as "Darwin (planet), Aristotle, Delta Pavonis (system)", which read equally well as an alternate name for the planet or as a body of its own. It is neither: Aristotle is what Orion's Arm calls Delta Pavonis itself.

**What the map does now.** The system is labelled Aristotle, by the rule that a primary with a name of its own gives the system its name. Darwin is its planet, and the catalogue designations stay on the star's panel.

**What would settle it.** Settled.
